# Corrections

Every factual claim in GeoGlobe was checked against published sources in September 2026, and the tool's text was corrected wherever a source contradicted it. This file records what changed and why. The sources themselves are numbered on [sources.html](sources.html), and each panel in the tool links to the ones behind it.

Sourcing ran in tiers: peer-reviewed papers and monographs first, then geological surveys, museums, park services and universities. Science press was used for framing only and never as the sole source for a number. Encyclopaedias, blogs and dealers were not cited. A claim that could not be traced to the first two tiers was removed rather than left standing.

**130 changes in total: 119 corrections and 11 removals.**


## Removals

Claims taken out because no source at the first two tiers supported them.

- **COMPARISONS 'glossopteris' / body**  
  Removed: “Coalfields in Brazil, South Africa, Madagascar, India, Australia and Antarctica all carry Glossopteris, a Permian seed fern with tongue-shaped leaves....”  
  No tier 1 or 2 source supports seeds too large for wind. Taylor, Taylor and Ryberg (USGS OFR 2007-1047 SRP 082) measure glossopterid ovules about 2 mm long. Searched USGS, Geological Society and journal abstracts.
- **period ord / body[1]**  
  Removed: “On land the first plants, liverwort-like and only millimetres tall, take hold in damp ground.”  
  No tier 1 or 2 source gives a height. Wellman et al. 2003 establish the liverwort affinity from spores and sporangia fragments alone; whole-plant fossils do not appear for another 50 million years, so the size is an inference.  
  Source: Wellman, C. H. 2003
- **period ord / facts / Land plants**  
  Removed: “["Land plants","Millimetre-scale, liverwort-like, in wet ground only"]”  
  Same as the body: the size and the 'wet ground only' habitat are inferences that no opened source states.  
  Source: Wellman, C. H. 2003
- **period per / body[1]**  
  Removed: “the interior holds sand seas thousands of kilometres across, preserved today”  
  No tier 1 or 2 source found for the dimension. Searched for Permian erg extents (Coconino, Rotliegend, Southern Permian Basin) before the session's shared search budget ran out; nothing opened gives a width.
- **period jur / body[0]**  
  Removed: “This is the only interval when both names apply at once.”  
  No source makes this claim, and usage runs against it: Kutzbach and Gallimore (1989) describe Pangaea at 250 to 200 Ma as 'the large bipolar land masses of Laurasia and Gondwanaland', and Gondwana is named from the Cambrian onward in this tool's own text.  
  Source: Kutzbach, J. E. 1989
- **period dev / body**  
  Removed: “Plants grow wood and roots, and by the end of the period forests stand thirty metres tall, the height of a ten-storey building.”  
  No opened tier 1 or 2 source gives a thirty-metre forest height; the sources read give 8 to 10 m for the Gilboa trees.
- **period pgn / body**  
  Removed: “India reaches Asia and the Himalaya starts to rise; weathering of the new mountains will pull carbon dioxide out of the air and help cool the planet o...”  
  The uplift-weathering link is a debated hypothesis (Raymo and Ruddiman 1992) and the paper could not be opened, so the claim is removed.
- **period tri / body**  
  Removed: “Life takes several million years to recover from the end-Permian extinction, and the groups that emerge are new, among them”  
  The recovery time rests on Chen and Benton 2012, which could not be opened.
- **js/terrain.js / ARID_OVERRIDES.tri / American southwest**  
  Removed: “{ plate:"NAM", name:"Pangaean interior, American southwest", source:"Moenkopi red beds and the Wingate dune sandstone", pts:[[-118,42],[-104,42],[-100...”  
  Parrish 1993 describes the Colorado Plateau as relatively wet though seasonal in the Triassic. The tool draws the Triassic at 220 Ma, which is Chinle time, and its own Petrified Forest site now calls that ground a warm humid lowland, so the arid patch contradicted both the source and the tool.  
  Source: Parrish, J. T. 1993
- **period pgn / site 'Green River lakes' / body**  
  Removed: “alongside more complete fossil fish than anywhere else on Earth”  
  no tier 1 or 2 source found for the global superlative after searching Crossref, Europe PMC and the National Park Service pages for Fossil Butte; the NPS calls the fossils famous for diversity, abundance and preservation and lists 27 fish species, with no comparison to other sites

## Still unsettled

These were checked but could not be settled, because the research ran out of its web-search budget or because the source sits behind a paywall with no readable abstract. They are listed here rather than quietly left alone.

- **Jehol Biota, Axel Heiberg and most of the Congo Basin site.** Every dating and preservation paper reachable for these was paywalled with no abstract in Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or Europe PMC.
- **The Gilboa forest age of 386 million years.** Stein et al. 2020 make the nearby Cairo forest the oldest known, and Khudadad 2021 puts Cairo two to three million years before Gilboa, which would place Gilboa nearer 382 Ma. No source states a Gilboa figure outright.
- **The Caledonian collision.** Its date of about 425 million years, the comparison with the Himalaya, and the palaeolatitude of 30 degrees south all went unsourced. McKerrow et al. 2000 warn that “Caledonian” covers some 200 million years.
- **Tiktaalik's length.** The 2.7 metre figure comes from two University of Chicago pages. No length appears in any Tiktaalik paper reachable in this session.
- **Saharan glacial pavements.** Deynoux and Ghienne 2004 read the striated surfaces as shear planes inside unlithified sand rather than ice-abraded bedrock, and work in Mauritania and Libya rather than southern Algeria.
- **Silurian sea scorpions.** The two-metre and largest-arthropod claims hold, but the 2.5 metre record holder Jaekelopterus is Early Devonian, and Anderson et al. 2014 argue Acutiramus had poor eyesight and may have fed in low light.
- **The South Pole site.** The line about the nearest plants being 2,000 km away on the peninsula is probably wrong on both count and place, since mosses grow on nunataks much closer, but no source states a southern limit.
- **Messel.** The recent literature explains the preservation by permanent stratification of the lake rather than by gas releases killing animals at the surface.
- **Several shallow seas and ocean names.** About ten entries in SEAS, and the periods in which Rheic, Paleo-Tethys, Tethys and Panthalassa are used, were not reached before the search budget ran out.
- **Most descriptions of what a place is like today.** Vegetation, rainfall and elevation details for a dozen sites could not be traced to a survey, museum or park service page.

## Known tensions

Places where the text and the globe's own geometry disagree, and the geometry was left alone.
The plate poses were solved and checked numerically before this pass, so correcting the text
against a published reconstruction can pull it away from where the tool actually draws the
marker. The README already says positions are schematic, and more so before 200 Ma.

Both of these are now stated in the tool itself. The site gives the published figure and the
figure this globe draws, side by side, and the About dialog explains why they differ.

**Rhynie sits at 25 degrees south in the text and 9 degrees south on the globe.** Wellman 2018
places the Rhynie chert at about 25 degrees south, and the site text was corrected to match.
The tool's Devonian poses plot Aberdeenshire at about 9 degrees south, and southern England at
about 16. The gap is wider than the ten degrees this pass treated as agreement. The arid patch
over Britain is unaffected and reads correctly either way: at 25 degrees south the belt model
would call that ground desert on its own, and at 9 degrees south it would call it tropical
forest, which is exactly the case the patch exists to override.


**The Silurian labels.** At the tool's Silurian moment of 425 million years ago, Cocks and Torsvik
2005 show Laurentia and Baltica already joined as Laurussia with the Iapetus closed, while this
globe draws about 20 degrees of water between Newfoundland and Scotland and labels the two
continents separately. The plate poses were left alone, so the labels were left to match them.
The Caledonian collision site now states both readings.
